Everyman’s Rights: Free Access to Nature

Finnish Culture · Guide
Table of contents

Few things define Finnish life more than jokamiehenoikeus — everyman’s right. It grants everyone, visitor and local alike, the freedom to roam, camp and forage across the vast Finnish landscape.

It is not a law written in fine print; it is a birthright, taught to children and practised daily, rooted in respect for nature and for private property.

What the right means

Everyman’s right lets you move freely on foot, ski, cycle and swim across forest and open land, and camp temporarily — regardless of ownership — as long as you do no harm. This is why Finland feels so wonderfully open.

It also grants you to pick wild berries, mushrooms and flowers, and to use the local water. The right is enjoyed by everyone, which is why it is guarded with real care.

Where you may go

  • Walk, ski and cycle on forest and open land
  • Camp overnight on any land, away from homes
  • Swim in lakes and seas
  • Pick berries, mushrooms and wildflowers
  • Cross unfenced land and use established paths

The freedom is breathtaking — but it comes with clear limits. You may not harm the environment, disturb the peace, cut growing trees, or use land in ways that trouble the owner.

What to respect

Everyman’s right has its mirror: everyman’s duty. Never enter gardens or yards, never camp beside someone’s house, never light a fire without permission or in the dry season, never litter, and never take more than you need.

The right is a trust. Nature is treated as shared wealth — yours to enjoy, never yours to spoil.

Teaching at school

Finnish children learn everyman’s right at school, early and honestly, alongside lessons on nature, foraging and campcraft. By adulthood, respecting the land and the right is simply second nature.

For visitors, the message is simple and joyful: step gently into the forest, take only what you will eat, and leave nothing but footprints. That is how the right stays sacred.

Frequently asked questions

Can I camp anywhere in Finland?

Almost — you may camp overnight in most places, away from homes and gardens, leaving no trace. A simple, quiet overnight is the classic use of the right.

Can I make a campfire?

Only where it is allowed and safe — never in the dry summer without permission. Many use portable stoves instead, which fall under the right more freely. When in doubt, ask.

Do I need permission to pick berries?

No. That is the beauty of it: wild berries and mushrooms may be picked by anyone, anywhere, under everyman’s right.
